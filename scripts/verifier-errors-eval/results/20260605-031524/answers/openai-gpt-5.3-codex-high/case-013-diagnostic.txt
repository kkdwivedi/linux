The failure is a **stack-depth limit** issue, not a type/ptr safety issue.

### Why verifier rejects it

BPF has a hard limit of **512 bytes combined stack across active call frames**.  
In your program:

- `helper_c()` allocates `buf[MAX_STACK]`
- `helper_b()` allocates `buf[MAX_STACK]`
- (and `helper_d()` also does, for another path)

With `MAX_STACK = 260`, each of those frames becomes larger than 260 due to alignment/compiler layout, and on the call chain:

`entry -> helper_c -> helper_b`

the verifier computes **544 bytes total**, which exceeds 512.

---

### Smallest likely fix

Reduce per-function stack allocation so nested `helper_c + helper_b` (and `helper_c + helper_d`) stay within 512.

A one-line fix is:

```diff
-#define MAX_STACK 260
+#define MAX_STACK 248
```

This should drop each heavy frame enough to bring the combined chain to (about) 512 or less.

---

### Patch

```diff
--- a/case.c
+++ b/case.c
```
